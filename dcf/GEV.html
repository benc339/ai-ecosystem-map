3. The money equation

The required bridge is from paid orders to recognized deliveries and then to repeatable cash. The factory map leaves equipment content and dollar content per campus load open. No GEV allocation, realized-price schedule or cash-conversion bridge is bundled. GAP: which physical production constraint binds GEV is not established. Industry lead times cannot substitute for company revenue.

Identity: Revenue = the sum of recognized equipment units x realized revenue per unit, plus service and software revenue, less eliminations. Cash flow from operations less capex = operating cash inflow minus operating cash outflow minus capital expenditure.

7. What could break it

These are operating and cash mechanisms, not a second count of the valuation hurdle. Their company-specific exposure is unquantified.

  • Delivery and acceptance delays — Supply constraints or commissioning delays could postpone recognition and collections even when customers still want equipment.
  • Customer project attrition — Financing, fuel supply, permits or grid access could prevent a planned campus from becoming a firm equipment order. This is demand conversion, not GEV manufacturing delay.
  • Contract execution costs — Input-cost increases, rework or warranty obligations could reduce profit on equipment already sold. A large order book would not by itself protect cash margins.
  • Cash-timing reversal — If advances or other working-capital inflows explain the cash wedge, later fulfillment could consume cash without equivalent new collections. The missing reconciliation prevents treating the latest cash margin as durable.
