AMD (AMD): how it makes money, why it'll make more, and what's priced in

As of 2026-10-23 12:37 ET. At 518.74 USD/share1, the unchanged consensus path requires a 43.7%2 CFO-minus-capex margin (valuation as of 2026-10-23; dcf/out/AMD.json and the inherited reverse-DCF registry in dcf/onepager/AMD.json). 1. How AMD makes money

The profit split is unknown. AMD sells AI accelerators and server processors, but the bundle does not show filed revenue or operating profit by segment (plan/cards/amd.md; dcf/out/AMD.json, inputs.segments). GAP: segment revenue and operating income; see dcf-gap:AMD:segments and dcf-gap:AMD:segment_op_income. Consolidated revenue was 34.6B USD3, operating margin was 10.7%4, and the CFO-minus-capex margin was 15.9%5 (FY ended 2025-12-27; dcf/out/AMD.json, revenue and margin inputs). These company totals do not establish accelerator profitability.

3. The money equation

The cash equation is revenue times the modeled consolidated CFO-minus-capex margin. A GW announcement does not supply the unit term. Converting it requires a power basis, product mix, shipment window and realized selling price. Rack and processor sales must not count the same billed hardware twice. GAP: the bundle cannot show whether qualified supply, customer acceptance or funding binds first; see dcf-gap:AMD:gw_units_asp_bridge and dcf-gap:AMD:qualified_supply (plan/cards/amd.md; plan/ANCHORS.md).

Identity: Revenue = accepted accelerator units x realized accelerator price + accepted server-processor units x realized processor price + other product revenue.

5. What's priced in

At 518.74 USD/share1 (2026-10-23; dcf/out/AMD.json, inputs.price), the unchanged consensus revenue path and model discount rate require a steady CFO-minus-capex margin of 43.7%2 (valuation as of 2026-10-23; dcf/onepager/AMD.json, reverse-DCF registry). That is above the last-FY margin of 15.9%5 and the historical range of 4.9%17 to 15.9%18 (FY ended 2025-12-27 and trailing annual history; dcf/out/AMD.json, margin inputs). Keeping the last-FY margin instead requires scaling the forecast revenue path by 2.47x19. FY2027 revenue then becomes 220.3B USD20, versus consensus of 89.2B USD21 (FY2027; dcf/onepager/AMD.json, reverse-DCF registry, and dcf/out/AMD.json, consensus inputs). Alternatively, the model discount rate must fall to 8.2%22 from 14.4%23 (valuation as of 2026-10-23; dcf/onepager/AMD.json, reverse-DCF and CAPM entries). These are separate model-equivalent solutions, not forecasts or proof of investor beliefs. Combinations can also reconcile the price. Consensus is an estimate path; price is what is paid.

7. What could break it

These are distinct operating failure modes, not repeated versions of the valuation gap. They can interact. No independent loss probabilities or additive valuation haircuts are assumed.

  • Supply readiness — Memory qualification, advanced packaging or rack integration could delay sellable output even with funded demand. Specifications prove content, not available capacity. The AMD-specific supply bridge is open (plan/cards/amd.md; the bundled ticker map).
  • Demand conversion — Customer funding, deployment readiness or competing products could reduce actual orders below announced ceilings. The anchor also flags warrant-linked demand; terms and dilution are not bundled. This concerns paid demand even when hardware is available (plan/ANCHORS.md, AMD row).
  • Cash capture — Even accepted sales could absorb inventory and receivables or carry weaker margins after supplier costs and product mix. The missing cash-flow reconciliation prevents measuring this risk. This concerns cash per sale, not whether the sale occurs (dcf/out/AMD.json, fcf_margin_forward gap).

8. The differentiated view

(a) What the price says the market believes. Under the inherited model, the price is equivalent to a 43.7%2 cash margin at the consensus path, or 2.47x19 times that revenue path at the last-FY margin (valuation as of 2026-10-23; dcf/onepager/AMD.json, reverse-DCF registry). These are conditional hurdles, not an observed market consensus about margins.

(b) Our variant. none established: the bundle shows product content and deployment ambitions, but no verified shipment, pricing or cash-margin advantage over what the market already anticipates.

(c) Why the market is wrong. Not established. The price is demanding under the inherited model, but a durable change in product mix or a lower required return could explain the gap. Public targets and unchecked supply-chain relays do not prove mispricing.

10. Takeaway

Pass. Product specifications and deployment targets outline a route to growth, not proof of cash generation. The bundled quote of 518.74 USD/share1 exceeds the bundled bull value of 437.93 USD/share26 (valuation as of 2026-10-23; dcf/out/AMD.json, inputs.price and scenarios.bull). That is a demanding model hurdle, not sufficient evidence for a short. Until accepted shipments, segment profit and cash conversion connect, neither an investment edge nor a free growth kicker is established.
