3. The money equation

Qualified InP supply binds the optical unit term in the factory account. A capacity multiple cannot be converted into module revenue without devices per module, yield, customer qualification, product mix, outside supply and realized selling prices. Internal lasers embedded in company modules must not be counted again as external component revenue. The pack has neither a matched company-unit and ASP pair nor a factory-tagged forward cash-margin input in the frozen DCF.

Identity: Consolidated revenue = external transceiver units x realized ASP + external laser and component units x realized ASP + other external product revenue. CFO-minus-capex = consolidated net earnings + noncash adjustments - net operating cash investment - cash capex. Internal transfers are eliminated. When using a CFO-minus-capex margin, do not subtract capex again.

7. What could break it

These are operating and financing failure modes, not repetitions of the reverse-DCF margin hurdle.

  • Qualified output misses the capacity roadmap — Wafer output can grow while usable laser yields, device qualification or customer acceptance delay shipments. Capacity headlines alone do not establish saleable modules.
  • Large customers capture the shortage economics — Unnamed customers accounted for 20.0%53 and 12.0%54 of FY2026 revenue. Purchase commitments and capacity rights may improve visibility while limiting repricing. The pack does not identify these customers or establish contract profitability.
  • Growth keeps consuming cash — Inventory and receivables may continue expanding before collections, while equipment bills fall due. Payables support is not permanent funding. Further financing could dilute shareholders even with successful shipments.
  • Architecture or product mix reduces captured value — A change in optical architecture could alter laser content, module mix or realized prices. Rising network bandwidth would then fail to translate proportionally into Coherent revenue and profit.
