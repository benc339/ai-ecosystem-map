3. The money equation

For each product, recognized units and realized price drive revenue. A factory bridge would connect customer MW to product units, Eaton capture share and shipment timing. It must separate IT load from facility power and avoid counting the same cooling or electrical content twice. Sector lead times point to a delivery constraint; Eaton-specific capacity utilization is unknown. GAP: attach quantities, prices, customer allocation and timing; dcf-gap:ETN:ai_revenue_attach. Soft power-equipment themes remain unvalued candidates.

Identity: Group revenue = sum of recognized product units x realized net price across non-overlapping product lines + remaining revenue. Product units = relevant customer deployment x units per deployment x Eaton capture share, shifted to the revenue-recognition period.

7. What could break it

These risks concern operating cash and model reliability. They are separate from the demanding valuation already described.

  • Customer readiness delays — A customer can defer an equipment delivery while waiting for site power or installation. Revenue recognition and cash collection then move later, even when the sector remains busy. The bundle does not quantify Eaton order cancellation terms.
  • Product qualification and mix — Cooling qualification, customer design choices or competing suppliers can reduce Eaton content or margin per deployment. Growth in customer MW need not become equivalent profit growth.
  • Working capital and reinvestment burden — Even with on-time deliveries, inventory buffers, receivable terms and capacity spending can make cash lag reported profit. An accounting margin improvement may therefore fail to lift the modeled cash margin. The cash-conversion bridge is missing.
  • Debt and valuation-method risk — The balance sheet carries net debt while the model ignores debt weight and discounts a cash measure that is not unlevered. Refinancing terms and a consistent equity-versus-enterprise cash-flow treatment could change valuation without any change in factory demand.
