3. The money equation

The missing conversion from chip workload to paid tester deliveries is the binding research gap. Workload depends on chip volumes, test insertions and test duration. Effective tester throughput depends on parallel sites, usable time and utilization. New orders also depend on the installed fleet, replacements and vendor share. Production-capacity targets are not shipments. KYEC procurement sets are not tester systems and cannot supply an ASP denominator. The pack identifies packaging and wafer output as constraints, but contains no primary confirmation that tester capacity is currently stopping accelerator shipments.

Identity: Revenue = SoC systems delivered x configured SoC ASP + memory systems delivered x configured memory ASP + other hardware, software and services revenue. Required active tester capacity = chip test volume x insertions per chip x seconds per insertion / (parallel sites x available tester seconds x utilization).

7. What could break it

These are operating and cash risks, separate from the valuation hurdles above.

  • Customers cannot convert AI demand into testable chips — Delayed wafer or advanced-packaging output pushes back equipment acceptance and test workloads, even when end demand remains strong.
  • More throughput per tester — Parallel testing, shorter test programs and better utilization can absorb chip growth without proportionate system purchases.
  • Platform share shifts — A competing qualified test platform can capture new designs. Growth in custom chips is not automatically adverse, because the pack already reports rising ASIC applications.
  • Expansion consumes cash before acceptance — Component commitments, inventory and receivables can grow before customers pay. Capacity buffers can become excess capacity if deliveries slip.
  • Strategic investment returns disappoint — Security purchases consume cash outside the model's capex deduction. Valuation gains and asset sales need not repeat or translate into recurring operating cash.
