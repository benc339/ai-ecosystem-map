3. The money equation

Use delivered, billable chips and retained economics, not announced power. ToAlpha estimates 5.1M units16 Broadcom TPU units (CY2027 forecast dated 2026-10-16; plan/cards/broadcom.md). This is not disclosed shipment volume. The bundle lacks customer-level prices, pass-through content, acceptance timing and funding. It cannot establish which constraint binds first. Supplier purchases are not customer demand, and the power target cannot be converted into revenue without a sourced bridge.

Identity: Revenue = accepted custom-chip units times recognized revenue per unit + networking units times realized selling price + other semiconductor revenue + recognized software license, subscription and service revenue. Operating profit = revenue minus product, service and operating costs. CFO less capex = net income + noncash adjustments - working-capital investment + other operating cash adjustments - capex. Customer-financing cash flows outside CFO require a separate equity-cash bridge.

That is risk-bearing customer finance, not cash already spent, revenue or a certain loss. Production spending can repay itself through customer collections; the bundle does not prove that payback. Customer funding is more speculative until terms and recoveries are known. Do not sum overlapping financing reports or subtract entire commitments from value as though they were losses. 7. What could break it

These are distinct operating and cash-loss channels, not additional labels for the valuation haircut. The delivery and funding questions come from plan/cards/broadcom.md and plan/ANCHORS.md.

  • Delivery and qualification delays — Memory, advanced packaging, supplier qualification or site readiness may delay accepted chips. The connection map leaves chip counts and packaging allocation open. This is a shipment-timing risk, not a forecast of customer default.
  • Customer-financing losses — Credit support can turn reported demand into lender exposure. Delayed repayment, recourse or further advances can reduce cash available to equity even if chips ship. The reported facility ceiling is not a measured loss; plan/cards/broadcom.md.
  • Supplier commitment mismatch — Unconditional purchases may come due before customer collections or survive a demand reduction. Inventory and cash can build while expected sales fail to arrive. This is a procurement obligation, separate from loans to customers; plan/cards/broadcom.md.
  • Customer bargaining and mix — Customers may shift design work or retain more value, while a larger hardware share may dilute consolidated cash margins. Unit growth and bandwidth do not establish profit growth. Missing segment economics prevent a reliable offset from software; plan/ANCHORS.md and dcf/out/AVGO.json.

(c) Why the market is wrong. No supported market error is established. Low reported capex does not capture supplier cash demands or customer-credit exposure. The bundle lacks draw schedules, recoverability and a segment profit bridge. These omissions justify more research, not a contrarian trade.

(e) Steelman. The bullish case is that custom-chip demand converts to cash, networking strengthens retained value and software supports profitability. The model-implied margin is already below the historical floor, so poor outcomes need not follow from financing capacity alone. The bearish reply is that the revenue fade may be too generous and that historically strong consolidated cash conversion may not survive customer financing and hardware mix.

Right-but-early risk. Delivery growth and new financing can support reported sales before repayment or margin problems appear. Passing can miss upside while the cash questions remain unresolved. Funding concerns alone do not time a short.

Falsifier. The optimistic conversion mechanism fails if accepted chip deliveries rise while cash collections net of incremental customer funding deteriorate persistently. Reconsider the pass only when filed cash bridges and verified allocations establish profitable delivery that consensus misses under a normalized revenue fade.

(f) Core vs free kicker. The operating core is software and semiconductor cash generation. Networking upgrades and wider deployment are potential growth sources, but this page assigns them no separate incremental value. They may already sit in consensus and are not demonstrably free.

Not betting on. No bet on converting announced power directly into chip units, treating supplier commitments as customer orders, valuing credit ceilings as revenue, or adding overlapping financing reports.

10. Takeaway

Pass. The reverse DCF allows weaker cash margins than the supplied annual history, so this is not a case that price requires record profitability. But the apparent cushion rests on a boom-growth fade and a cash measure that does not fully capture customer financing. The factory evidence supports business opportunity, not a demonstrated pricing error. Require a segment profit bridge, funded deliveries and a financing-adjusted cash reconciliation before taking a directional view.
