3. The money equation

For power generation, delivered energy and realized net spread matter more than announced capacity. The pack supplies contract capacity but no annual energy, price or project margin. The binding analytical gap is the conversion from contracted power to incremental cash after displaced sales and required spending. There is no factory-tagged forward margin or capex input in this DCF.

Identity: Generation revenue = delivered MWh x realized USD per MWh + capacity and ancillary-service revenue. Delivered MWh = available MW x delivery hours x utilization. Consolidated revenue = external generation sales + external retail sales + other recognized revenue, after internal-sale eliminations and reported hedge effects. Incremental cash = incremental realized revenue minus fuel, purchased power, operating costs, taxes, working capital and required cash investment.

7. What could break it

These are operating, timing and financing mechanisms, not repeated claims that the valuation is demanding.

  • Outages and adverse power spreads — Generation outages or weaker energy spreads reduce cash earnings. Retail obligations can require replacement purchases when the fleet is unavailable.
  • Hedge collateral liquidity — Hedges can require cash collateral before the related generation cash arrives. Earnings protection does not eliminate liquidity stress.
  • Contract delivery and counterparty execution — The New Era filing makes supply conditional on infrastructure arrangements and credit support. Announced demand may not become billed load on schedule. Nuclear uprates also require execution.
  • Capital deployment and competing claims — Acquisitions, maintenance, environmental spending and financing claims can absorb cash attributed to common equity. Historical cash margins do not separately forecast acquisition economics.

10. Takeaway

Vistra has a credible route from power demand to cash through existing generation, retail and signed contracts. The price nevertheless requires cash conversion above the model's historical normalization. It does not require an unprecedented historical margin. The factory pack provides contract context but no priced cash-return advantage, and the valuation engine needs a consistent leverage treatment. Pass until delivered-energy economics and all-in cash returns can be reconciled to a sound valuation.
