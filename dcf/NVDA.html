NVIDIA (NVDA): how it makes money, why it'll make more, and what's priced in

As of 2026-10-23 18:34 ET. At 212.07 USD/share1 the market needs a 50.7%2 steady FCF margin on the consensus revenue path, above last year's {dcf:fcf_margin_last} and the four-year high {dcf:fcf_margin_hist_max}; factory evidence says the near-term revenue is supply-capped, so we pass.

1. How NVIDIA makes money

NVIDIA sells accelerated-computing systems (GPUs, then whole racks with networking) mostly to cloud and AI builders. Data Center was 89.7%3 of revenue last fiscal year (193.7B USD4 of 215.9B USD5). Company-level profit is extreme: operating margin 60.4%6. The segment PROFIT split is not yet in the model, so it is shown as a gap rather than guessed.

3. The money equation

One identity explains almost all of it.

Identity: Data Center revenue = GW of AI capacity shipped x NVIDIA dollars per GW. In the near term the GW term is set by CoWoS packaging and HBM supply, not by orders.

4. Where the money goes

Spending is light on factories and heavy on R&D, supply prepayments and strategic investments; NVIDIA has committed 10.0B USD19 to European AI companies and institutions (factory card, neoclouds lane). The split between investments that pay back and backstops for neoclouds is not sourced yet.

5. What's priced in

Consensus is an estimate path; the price is what is paid. Holding our DCF engine fixed, the price of 212.07 USD/share1 implies either a steady FCF margin of 50.7%2 (vs 44.8%9 last year and a four-year high of 46.6%20), or consensus revenue about 1.05x21 today's path at last year's margin, or a cost of equity of 12.9%22 instead of 13.4%23. Our base case is 201.87 USD/share24, so the stock trades 5.1%25 above it.

7. What could break it

Each risk is distinct; none repeats a priced-in point.

  • Supply cap binds harder — If CoWoS or HBM output slips, the FY28 growth guide of 70.0%34 is the ceiling, not the floor
  • Margin normalisation — Custom chips (TPU, Trainium, Maia) and AMD give buyers leverage; every point of FCF margin moves value about one for one (see gaps)
  • Customer financing loop — Part of demand is backstopped by NVIDIA itself (neocloud equity and capacity guarantees); if that share grows, reported demand overstates end demand

8. The differentiated view

(a) What the price says the market believes. The price says NVIDIA sustains a 50.7%2 FCF margin on consensus revenue, or revenue 1.05x21 the consensus path at today's margin.

(b) Our variant. The near-term revenue path is a supply number, not a demand number. New demand (for example SpaceX) re-allocates capped units between buyers rather than adding upside, so the extra revenue the price needs has to come from price and mix, i.e. margin.

(c) Why the market is wrong. Bulls count SpaceX GW as additive to the supply-constrained growth guide; management called that guide supply-constrained. Additive counting double-books the same CoWoS/HBM capacity.

10. Takeaway

NVIDIA is a supply-limited machine with near-peak margins. The price already asks for FCF margins above anything in the last four years; factory evidence says extra demand cannot add units in FY28, so the bet is purely on price/mix. Pass until the forward margin gap is sourced.
