# NVIDIA (NVDA): how it makes money, why it'll make more, and what's priced in

*As of 2026-10-23 07:10 ET. At 212.21 USD/share[^1] the market needs a 50.7%[^2] steady FCF margin on the consensus revenue path, above last year's {dcf:fcf_margin_last} and the four-year high {dcf:fcf_margin_hist_max}; factory evidence says the near-term revenue is supply-capped, so we pass.*

## 1. How NVIDIA makes money

NVIDIA sells accelerated-computing systems (GPUs, then whole racks with networking) mostly to cloud and AI builders. Data Center was 89.7%[^3] of revenue last fiscal year (193.7B USD[^4] of 215.9B USD[^5]). Company-level profit is extreme: operating margin 60.4%[^6]. The segment PROFIT split is not yet in the model, so it is shown as a gap rather than guessed.

Capex is small (6.0B USD[^13]) because TSMC and the memory makers carry the fabs. Net income, SBC and working-capital lines are not in the model yet (gap dcf-gap:NVDA:ni_sbc_wc).

## 3. The money equation

One identity explains almost all of it.

**Identity:** Data Center revenue = GW of AI capacity shipped x NVIDIA dollars per GW. In the near term the GW term is set by CoWoS packaging and HBM supply, not by orders.

## 4. Where the money goes

Spending is light on factories and heavy on R&D, supply prepayments and strategic investments; NVIDIA has committed 10.0B USD[^21] to European AI companies and institutions (factory card, neoclouds lane). The split between investments that pay back and backstops for neoclouds is not sourced yet.

## 5. What's priced in

Consensus is an estimate path; the price is what is paid. Holding our DCF engine fixed, the price of 212.21 USD/share[^1] implies either a steady FCF margin of 50.7%[^2] (vs 44.8%[^11] last year and a four-year high of 46.6%[^22]), or consensus revenue about 1.13x[^23] today's path at last year's margin, or a cost of equity of 12.3%[^24] instead of 13.4%[^25]. Our base case is 187.33 USD/share[^26], so the stock trades 13.3%[^27] above it.

## 7. What could break it

Each risk is distinct; none repeats a priced-in point.

- **Supply cap binds harder** — If CoWoS or HBM output slips, the FY28 growth guide of 70.0%[^36] is the ceiling, not the floor
- **Margin normalisation** — Custom chips (TPU, Trainium, Maia) and AMD give buyers leverage; every point of FCF margin moves value about one for one (see gaps)
- **Customer financing loop** — Part of demand is backstopped by NVIDIA itself (neocloud equity and capacity guarantees); if that share grows, reported demand overstates end demand

## 8. The differentiated view

**(a) What the price says the market believes.** The price says NVIDIA sustains a 50.7%[^2] FCF margin on consensus revenue, or revenue 1.13x[^23] the consensus path at today's margin.

**(b) Our variant.** The near-term revenue path is a supply number, not a demand number. New demand (for example SpaceX) re-allocates capped units between buyers rather than adding upside, so the extra revenue the price needs has to come from price and mix, i.e. margin.

**(c) Why the market is wrong.** Bulls count SpaceX GW as additive to the supply-constrained growth guide; management called that guide supply-constrained. Additive counting double-books the same CoWoS/HBM capacity.

**Factory evidence:**
- `plan/theses/NVDA-MEMORY-SPACEX-2027.md` — SpaceX de-risks the FY28 guide of 70.0%[^36] but cannot lift revenue above CoWoS/HBM limits; upside only via price/mix
- `plan/cards/nvidia.md` — HBM per GPU rises with Rubin, so HBM bits, not orders, set units
- `dcf-gap:NVDA:fcf_margin_forward` — The forward margin, the input the price hinges on, is still unsourced

**Catalyst:** NVIDIA Q3 FY27 results and FY28 outlook (2026-11)

| Claim that must be true | P |
|---|---|
| FY28 units are capped by CoWoS/HBM, not demand | 0.70 |
| Price/mix cannot lift FCF margin to the implied level | 0.50 |
| **Blended** | **0.35 → pass** |

**(e) Steelman.** Rubin content per GW is well above Blackwell, so price/mix alone could close the gap; and the price may be a correct net of a lower margin and a longer runway than consensus models.

**Right-but-early risk.** Even if the margin bar is too high, a strong Q3 print and Rubin pull-in can carry the stock for quarters before margins are tested.

**Falsifier.** NVIDIA guides FY28 revenue above the supply-constrained figure (units, not price) or shows CoWoS/HBM capacity beyond what the factory HBM lane has, i.e. supply is not the binding term.

**(f) Core vs free kicker.** Core: the margin bar implied by the price. Free kicker: unconstrained demand if CoWoS/HBM capacity arrives early.

**Not betting on.** Not shorting NVIDIA and not betting that AI demand fades; the evidence shows demand above supply. The seductive frame (SpaceX GW adds straight to NVIDIA revenue) is the one we reject.

## 10. Takeaway

NVIDIA is a supply-limited machine with near-peak margins. The price already asks for FCF margins above anything in the last four years; factory evidence says extra demand cannot add units in FY28, so the bet is purely on price/mix. Pass until the forward margin gap is sourced.
