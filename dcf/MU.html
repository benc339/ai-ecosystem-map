3. The money equation

Qualified bits shipped and realized prices are the binding unknowns in this revenue bridge. The factory map supports multi-vendor qualification but leaves Micron allocation and shipped units open. It does not prove a quantified supply ceiling. Module density needs placement and price data before it becomes revenue. HBM wafer allocation may affect conventional DRAM supply, but neither that effect nor a NAND pricing benefit is measured (plan/cards/micron.md; plan/ANCHORS.md; dcf/L3-L4-TICKER-MAP.md).

Identity: Revenue = HBM bits shipped x HBM price per bit + non-HBM DRAM bits shipped x DRAM price per bit + NAND bits shipped x NAND price per bit.

7. What could break it

These are distinct operating channels, not repeated valuation discounts. No extra numerical haircut is applied.

  • Qualified output — Packaging or die-yield problems delay customer-qualified bits despite installed capacity and bookings. This reduces delivered volume even when demand holds.
  • Memory pricing — Rival supply or weaker conventional-memory demand lowers realized prices and raises inventory. This can reduce profit even when the HBM ramp executes.
  • Cash conversion — Spending, incentive timing and working capital consume cash before capacity earns a return. Operating profit can remain strong while CFO less capex falls.
  • Contract protection — Booked output may lack firm prices, cancellation penalties or payment protection. Customer reallocation can weaken Micron's realized economics without a general memory downturn.
