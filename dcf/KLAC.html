3. The money equation

Customer wafer-fab equipment budgets are not KLA revenue. A valid bridge needs process-control intensity, KLA capture and installation timing. Backlog timing is the visible constraint; the pack does not establish a quantified physical shipment ceiling. Tool counts and realized selling prices are missing. The bundled forward cash margin 30.3%38 is a historical-average modeling choice, not company guidance or analyst consensus. No factory-tagged annual capex or cash-margin forecast curve is present.

Identity: Revenue = sum across product families of recognized units x realized price + covered installed systems x service revenue per system + billable service work. CFO less capex = operating cash flow - KLA cash capital expenditures. Customer fab capex is a demand driver, not KLA's own cash spending.

7. What could break it

These operating mechanisms are separate from the valuation hurdle. Missing evidence is not proof that an adverse outcome will occur.

  • Customer readiness delays recognition — Cleanroom readiness, installation and acceptance delays push backlog into later periods even when customer demand remains intact.
  • Market access removes revenue — Export restrictions or loss of service access can eliminate addressable sales rather than merely delay them. No new restriction or impact estimate is assumed.
  • Tool intensity does not become KLA share — Customer process changes, competing tools or different inspection strategies can absorb the technology transition without the assumed increase in KLA revenue per fabrication step.
  • Profit growth consumes cash — Receivables and inventories can grow faster than collections. Falling current customer deposits do not prove cancellations, but they also do not fund the larger backlog automatically. Continued distributions can reduce financial flexibility.
