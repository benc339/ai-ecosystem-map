3. The money equation

HBM sales require accelerator demand, memory content, supplier allocation, accepted shipments and realized price to align. Raw wafer input is not saleable HBM output. The factory map flags qualification and shared packaging as possible gates, but does not establish which currently binds for SK hynix. GAP: the supplier-specific volume, yield and price bridge remains open.

Identity: Revenue = HBM good bits sold x realized price per bit + conventional DRAM good bits sold x realized price per bit + NAND good bits sold x realized price per bit.

4. Where the money goes

FY2025 capital spending was 28.6T KRW8, or 29.4%16 of revenue. The separate Yongin commitment 35.2T KRW15 covers a multiyear program; do not add it to annual capex. Qualified capacity with contracted demand could earn its cost, but the bundle does not prove project returns. Capacity built ahead of qualification carries execution risk. The shareholder-return target is above 50.0%17 of cumulative FCF during FY2025-FY2027. This is guidance, not completed distributions. Its FCF definition needs reconciliation to the model. Distributions use FCF; they are not another deduction in operating cash flow less capex. That is below FY2025 margin 25.5%6 and within the historical range from -13.7%18 to 25.5%19. Alternatively, holding the FY2025 margin fixed, the required future revenue path is 2.34x20 of consensus, with final consensus-year revenue 1,240.2T KRW21 versus FY2027 consensus 530.6T KRW22. Holding both the original revenue path and margin instead implies discount rate 7.1%23. These are alternative conditional solves, not simultaneous investor forecasts. Consensus is an estimate path; the share price is what is paid. The low implied margin is not proof of cheapness because the revenue fade carries boom growth forward.

7. What could break it

The operating risks below concern different failures in share, market pricing, manufacturing and cash timing. They are not additional valuation haircuts layered onto the reverse-DCF alternatives.

  • Customer reallocation after rival qualification. — Customers can move HBM orders to another qualified supplier even when total memory demand grows. Revenue-share estimates and future bit-share forecasts are not comparable measures.
  • Market-wide memory pricing. — A supply response or weaker demand can reduce realized prices and utilization across conventional memory. Company share can remain stable while cash generation falls.
  • Manufacturing yield and packaging execution. — Scrap, stacking failures or unavailable packaging can keep wafer starts from becoming accepted shipments. Nominal installed capacity then overstates usable supply.
  • Capital commitments outlast near-term receipts. — Facility spending and working capital can absorb cash before qualified products are delivered. Deferred customer ramps can therefore weaken cash conversion without an immediate collapse in reported operating margin.
