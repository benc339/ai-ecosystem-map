3. The money equation

The substrate business earns accepted shipment volume multiplied by realized selling price. Larger packages can consume more process capacity per shipment. Material availability, yield and customer qualification could bind, but the bundle does not identify Ibiden's binding constraint. GAP: the bridge from AI accelerator demand to company substrate revenue and cash. Do not add substrate revenue to a film supplier's revenue as though they were the same output.

Identity: Substrate revenue = customer-accepted substrate shipments x realized price per substrate. Consolidated revenue = substrate revenue + other business revenue. CFO minus capex = operating cash flow - cash capital expenditure.

7. What could break it

These are distinct operating risks. They are not repetitions of the valuation and solver problems above. They remain mechanisms to test, not quantified company outcomes.

  • Factory ramp and customer qualification — Installed capacity may not become accepted shipments if yield or qualification disappoints. Fixed costs then weigh on profit even with orders available.
  • ABF supply and input costs — The bundled factory map classifies Ibiden as a film buyer. Restricted allocation can cap shipments, while higher film costs can absorb selling-price gains.
  • Demand and utilization — Weaker customer orders or lost design positions can leave productive capacity underused. The bundle does not quantify customer concentration or committed demand.
  • Cash-cycle duration — Further capital spending or working-capital needs can keep free cash negative despite better accounting profit. That could delay shareholder cash returns.
