3. The money equation

This is an analytical identity, not a disclosed segment model. Count sold products without counting the same rack and its embedded components again. Delivery readiness and cash conversion are separate gates. GAP: the bundle does not identify Super Micro's binding component, accepted units, realized prices or collection cycle.

Identity: Revenue = sum of recognized product units x realized selling price, plus separately recognized service revenue. Cash from operations less capex = operating cash receipts - operating cash payments - capital spending.

7. What could break it

These are conditional business risks, not claims of observed incidents. They describe different failure mechanisms rather than repeating the valuation hurdle.

  • Delivery and acceptance delays — Unavailable accelerators or unfinished customer power and cooling can defer system acceptance and cash receipts. The bundled factory map supports the general mechanism, not a quantified Super Micro exposure.
  • Integration margin leakage — Competitive pricing, component costs or rework can reduce profit on systems that are delivered. Revenue growth need not improve retained profit.
  • Customer collection and funding — After delivery, slow payment or customer default can tie up or destroy cash. Financing purchases before collection can require external funding. The bundle does not quantify customer credit or funding needs.

10. Takeaway

Pass and not valued. The bundle establishes sales and accounting profit alongside cash consumption, but not why that cash will recover. The conditional margin hurdle is useful as a test, not as a forecast. The revenue and discount-rate reverse outputs cannot support an investment case at the retained negative cash margin. Require a filed profit-to-cash bridge, sourced forward cash conversion and company-specific unit economics before assigning fair value.
