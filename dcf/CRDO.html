3. The money equation

The missing link is monetization, not the existence of AI spending. Deployed racks must translate into qualified links, Credo allocation and realized selling prices. Longer optical links and shorter copper links should not be counted as identical units. The binding operating constraint cannot be identified from a calendar entry. The bundled DCF contains no factory-tagged capex guidance or forward cash-margin inputs.

Identity: AEC revenue = deployed racks x eligible links per rack x Credo unit share x realized revenue per cable. Consolidated revenue = AEC revenue + other product revenue + recognized IP license and royalty revenue. Count embedded silicon within a sold cable only once. CFO less capex = net income + noncash adjustments + signed changes in operating assets and liabilities - cash property and equipment purchases.

7. What could break it

These are operating and capital-allocation failure modes, separate from the valuation hurdle.

  • Customer deployment concentration — Customers A and B contributed 43.0%44 and 28.0%45 of latest-quarter revenue. These are filed customer labels, not inferred hyperscaler identities. Delayed qualifications or customer deployments can concentrate the shipment shortfall.
  • Architecture and supplier substitution — If optical links replace copper in an exposed application, AEC revenue can fall while total network spending rises. Competing designs can also reduce allocation or selling prices. New optical sales must be checked for substitution rather than counted as pure upside.
  • Supplier execution — Outsourced fabrication and cable assembly expose shipment timing to supplier capacity, yield and quality failures. This can delay recognized revenue even when qualified customer demand remains intact.
  • Cash absorption and dilution — Inventory and receivables absorb cash before collections. Equity compensation adds back 182.6M USD7 to annual CFO but is not economically free. A fixed share count can overstate future cash generation per share.
  • Acquired-product execution — The optical acquisition required 735.6M USD21 of net cash. Qualification delays or weak incremental profit can impair the return on that capital even if the existing cable business performs.
