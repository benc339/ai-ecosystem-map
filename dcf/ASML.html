7. What could break it

These are operating and execution risks, not repetitions of the valuation hurdle. The mechanisms are supported by the factory pack and ASML's interim risk disclosures: [https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828026048235/statutoryinterimreport20.htm](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828026048235/statutoryinterimreport20.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

  • Critical components delay scanner output — Optics or other specialized components can delay assembly and customer delivery. More customer demand then lengthens the queue instead of producing more recognized revenue.
  • Customers defer fab readiness or node adoption — Cleanroom readiness, process qualification or changes in node economics can delay equipment acceptance even when supplier capacity is available. A High-NA installed tool is not proof of broad production adoption.
  • Export restrictions change the addressable mix — Licensing changes can block systems or service work and redirect production toward a different mix. No incremental loss estimate is inferred from unsourced regional revenue shares.
  • Cash arrives in a different period from profit — Inventory, receivables, supplier payments and customer advances can pull cash conversion below reported earnings growth. Historic FCF must not be treated as a recurring forward margin without this bridge.
  • Technology and investment returns disappoint — High-NA development and strategic investments can consume cash before customers realize sufficient production benefits. Higher system revenue does not guarantee higher incremental cash profit.
