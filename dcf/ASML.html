7. What could break it

These are operating and cash mechanisms, separate from the price-implied expectations.

  • Critical-component or acceptance delays — Supplier bottlenecks, installation constraints or customer acceptance delays defer recognized revenue and cash even when demand exceeds available capacity.
  • Demand cancellation — Customer demand forecasts and interest in future orders are not irrevocable purchases. Weaker AI project economics or a memory downturn can delay fab equipment commitments.
  • Export and service restrictions — Restrictions can block shipments, upgrades or servicing of an installed fleet. Demand elsewhere need not replace the same product mix or cash contribution.
  • Product-mix and pricing economics — Higher-priced High-NA systems can carry immature margins. Productivity improvements may reduce tools required per wafer, while negotiated price increases may not fully offset development and manufacturing costs.
  • Customer advances and cash timing — Advance receipts, receivables, inventories and taxes can make annual cash margins depart from underlying profitability. Expansion spending can absorb cash before revenue is recognized.
