3. The money equation

The equipment term depends on orders converted into accepted shipments, product mix and realised selling prices. The service term depends on the installed base, utilisation and paid coverage. Q3 FY2026 systems mix was 67.0%27 foundry, logic and other, 26.0%28 DRAM and 7.0%32 flash. These shares partition the same segment. HBM packaging is included within DRAM; geography is a separate dimension. The pack has no matched tool-unit and ASP pair, so it cannot independently reproduce the consensus path. It does not establish a unique physical bottleneck for Applied.

Identity: Total revenue = sum of recognised tool shipments by product x realised ASP by product + service contracts and spare-parts revenue + Other revenue. CFO-minus-capex = net income + noncash adjustments + operating asset and liability changes - Applied's own capital spending. Customer fab capex is a demand input, not Applied's own capex.

7. What could break it

These are operating transmission risks rather than repetitions of the valuation premium.

  • Customer investment delays — Fab readiness or customer budget changes can defer tool acceptance and reduce service utilisation even when long-run semiconductor demand remains intact.
  • Process-position losses — Competing equipment or different process architectures can reduce Applied content per wafer despite growing customer capex. The factory pack does not quantify process-level share.
  • Trade and export restrictions — Licensing constraints can remove accessible tool and service sales. China represented 28.0%54 of consolidated Q3 FY2026 revenue; that is geographic exposure, not a claim that all such revenue is restricted or unrelated to AI.
  • Investment and cash-conversion shortfall — Lab and manufacturing expansion, inventory and receivables can absorb cash before new products pay back. Reported operating-profit growth would then overstate improvement in cash available after capital spending.
