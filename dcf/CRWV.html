7. What could break it

These are operating and financing failure paths, not additional valuation haircuts. They can interact but should not be counted twice.

  • Funding runs out before customer collections — Debt service, equipment obligations and leases can fall due before new capacity earns cash. A funding gap can force dilution, refinancing or delayed delivery even when customer demand remains intact (dcf/out/CRWV.json; plan/cards/coreweave.md).
  • Delivery misses change contract economics — Landlord readiness does not establish GPU acceptance or customer billing. The factory lease evidence distinguishes costly tenant termination from fee-free exit rights after missed delivery. Those landlord clauses do not establish matching customer protections (dcf/evidence/CRWV.json).
  • Customer concentration weakens cash durability — Microsoft represented 67.0%50 of revenue, not profit (FY2025; [https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000104/crwv-20251231.htm](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000104/crwv-20251231.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)). Customer payment problems, internal capacity or nonrenewal could reduce cash while site and financing obligations remain.
  • Replacement and renewal pricing erode cohort returns — Newer equipment can pressure renewal rates before older equipment is economically repaid. Strong prices on selected short-dated contracts do not establish durable fleet pricing or useful life. GAP: cohort returns and replacement spending (plan/cards/coreweave.md; dcf/evidence/CRWV.json).
