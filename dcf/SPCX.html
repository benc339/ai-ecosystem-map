3. The money equation

The missing link is monetization, not another headline capacity total. Nameplate, energized IT capacity and externally billed capacity are different measures. The pack supplies neither a realized compute price nor the paid allocation needed for an earnings bridge.

Identity: SI infrastructure revenue = time-average energized usable capacity x externally billable allocation x realized fee per capacity-period. Add software, model access and advertising separately. Starlink consumer service revenue = average paying subscribers x monthly ARPU x billed months; reconcile enterprise service and equipment separately. Space revenue = external customer launches x realized launch price plus development contracts. Internal launches are not external sales.

7. What could break it

Separate physical delivery, customer demand, unit returns and financing. Do not count the same cash shortfall as several independent risks.

  • Power and commissioning — Delivered accelerators cannot earn revenue before suitable power, cooling and commissioning. The factory distinguishes permitted or installed generation from usable IT capacity.
  • External revenue durability — Customer B supplies 34.5%49 of consolidated revenue and belongs to SI, implying about 66.5%50 of SI revenue. General cloud-service terms allow termination on 90 days51 notice. Customer B-specific terms and identity are not established.
  • Replacement economics — Lower realized compute pricing or faster hardware replacement can destroy cash returns even after capacity is energized and sold. Quarterly SI capex is 2.18x27 its revenue.
  • Starlink price dilution — Consumer ARPU fell from 83 USD/month23 to 69 USD/month24. Subscriber growth must cover that dilution and ongoing satellite replacement.
  • Funding and dilution — A fixed share count and simplified cash proxy do not model the cost of funding prolonged investment. Future equity issuance or combination terms could reduce per-share returns.
