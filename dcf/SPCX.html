SpaceX (SPCX): how it makes money, why it'll make more, and what's priced in

As of 2026-10-23 09:27 ET. SpaceX is now mostly an AI-compute company (SI is 51.9%1 of Q3 revenue). Our segment DCF gives 3.99 USD/share2 vs a price of 144.75 USD/share3, but management's Q1 2027 guide is far above our assumed growth, so the model, not the stock, is the first suspect.

1. How SpaceX makes money

Three businesses. Space sells launches (1.8B USD4 revenue, 357.0M USD5 segment EBITDA in Q3-26). Connectivity is Starlink (5.2B USD6 revenue, 3.5B USD7 EBITDA): subscribers went from 7.2M subscribers8 to 13.6M subscribers9 in a year while consumer ARPU fell from 83 USD/month10 to 69 USD/month11. SI (ex-xAI plus X) sells AI compute and ads (7.6B USD12 revenue, 5.0B USD13 EBITDA). Segment figures are quarterly (Q3-26); EBITDA is the company's non-GAAP segment measure, not operating income.

Cash wedge: Segment EBITDA is strong but capex eats it: SI spent 16.4B USD14 on capex in the quarter, 2.18x15 its revenue, and Q4 capex is guided to 35.0B USD16 to 40.0B USD17. Free cash flow is therefore negative today; the value is all in later years.

3. The money equation

One identity drives most of the value.

Identity: SI revenue = GW of compute online x revenue per GW, minus a capex bill per GW that is paid up front; Starlink (subscribers x ARPU) is the cash engine underneath.

4. Where the money goes

Almost all cash goes into AI data centers: SI capex was 16.4B USD14 in Q3-26, and the Q4 guide is 35.0B USD16 to 40.0B USD17. Net cash of 51.8B USD24 is the buffer. Payback claims (under a year at management's quoted pricing) are not yet sourced into the model.

5. What's priced in

Holding our segment engine fixed, the price of 144.75 USD/share3 needs one of: every segment's year-one growth 3.45x25 our base assumption; every segment's year-ten EBITDA margin 2.69x26 our base (impossible, it pushes margins above one hundred percent); or a discount rate of 4.2%27 instead of 8.8%28. Our base case is 3.99 USD/share2, so the price is 36.30x29 the base. The growth route is the only plausible one, and the company's own guide points that way.

7. What could break it

Each risk is distinct.

  • SI revenue quality — SI revenue jumped in one year and its customer and related-party split is not extracted; if a few buyers dominate, the guided growth is fragile
  • Capex without payback — Capex near 2.18x15 SI revenue only works if compute pricing holds; a price drop strands the capacity
  • Starlink ARPU dilution — ARPU fell from 83 USD/month10 to 69 USD/month11; the cash engine weakens if this continues faster than subscribers grow
  • Tesla combination — The 10-Q mentions evaluating a potential combination with Tesla; terms could dilute holders and are not modelled

8. The differentiated view

(a) What the price says the market believes. The price says SpaceX grows about 3.45x25 faster than our base paths (or is discounted at 4.2%27).

(b) Our variant. None established. The gap between our base and the price is mostly a stale-assumption problem: the company's Q1 2027 guide already sits 140.1%22 above the run-rate, which our base growth paths do not reflect.

(c) Why the market is wrong. Not shown. Until the segment paths are rebuilt from guidance we cannot say the market is wrong.

10. Takeaway

SpaceX is a Starlink cash engine funding a very large AI-compute build. Our segment DCF says 3.99 USD/share2 against 144.75 USD/share3, but its growth paths ignore the company's own guide. Pass until the segment paths are rebuilt from guidance and SI customer concentration is sourced.
