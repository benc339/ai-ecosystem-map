3. The money equation

Wafer-input capacity is not sellable stacks. Die yield, stack yield, qualification and customer allocation bind the conversion into revenue. The cited HBM catalog configuration holds 36 GB/stack20; it is a product specification, not average shipped mix. Price per bit and capacity per byte must use consistent units. GAP: usable dies per wafer, accepted stacks, realized selling prices and the memory-to-group profit bridge.

Identity: Consolidated revenue = external product units times realized selling prices, summed across businesses, plus external service revenue. HBM revenue = customer-accepted stacks times realized price per stack. Convert to reporting currency and remove internal sales. FCF = operating cash flow minus capex.

7. What could break it

These are distinct operating failure modes. They are not extra valuation discounts to stack onto the reverse DCF.

  • Qualification and manufacturing yield — Design qualification or announced mass production does not quantify accepted shipment yield. Defects and customer approval delays can prevent installed capacity from becoming paid output.
  • Memory price and contract reset — Even delivered output can earn less if supply improves or contracts reprice. Agreement coverage alone does not prove fixed prices, take-or-pay terms or attractive margins.
  • Investment and working-capital absorption — Even profitable shipments may fail to generate free cash when capex, inventory or receivables grow. Foundry spending can absorb memory cash before external utilization becomes profitable.
